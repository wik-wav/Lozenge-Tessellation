# Setup - Characters

# Setup - Characters of the Kjè Story

This note serves as documentation of the characters of the  Kjè story. The characters are categorized by their recurrence as well as impact on the plotline.

## Primary characters
#### Protagonist: The Projection

The Projection named Kjè  ([[kjè (root word)|kjè (Asaxi root word) - "of or related to trees"]] is a Giraffe of short stature (overall size and strength). Because of his physical state, he developed into a tracer-nightmare, but as with all natures, he is unaware of this. He is not keen on partaking in any activities that rely on strength, resilience or overall confidence in physical ability. For what he lacks in that department, he makes up for in conscientiousness and rigidity. He is like a homing missile, already fired and on track toward his target. As structured as he tries to be, things inevitably go awry and when they do, he becomes unpredictable and quite explosive. While he is aware of his pursuit of stability, deep down he yearns for connection, which requires ceasing control and taking note of emotion, balancing safety with risk.
Due to his nature for much of the story, he is not well liked. It remains unclear if he is still a nightmare after the story concludes.
Plausible deniability: Actions taken by The Projection usually stem from fear, not malice. In contexts where he lost control, his recklessness often results in events which trigger a deepening self-hatred festering within, even though there is no ill-will involved in the majority of the incidents in which he takes the role of a catalyst.

#### Deuteragonist: [[Lem the Shapeshifter|Lem the Shapeshifter]]

Lem takes the role of a helpful tracer-guide shapeshifter, but not overtly so. He is a recurring character, but each time appears in a different shape, recognized only by his clothing's colour scheme and specific accessories. He is very brave and as such, charismatic. Mostly all bark, no bite. The fact he is a tracer only surfaces once recognized by the Projection as Lem.

#### Contagonist: The environment of [[The Lozenge Tessellation|The Lozenge Tessellation]]'s dreamscape

Affected by state of affairs — sways either which way the action takes it. Often causes further chaos.

## Plot Devices

#### Extras

Appear in each chapter. Interact with the environment passively, until directly confronted. Once noticed, they attempt to maintain their routine. Given enough attention, they may snap out of it and cause turmoil or resolution in a brief moment of lucidity.

#### Nighthare 

See [[Natures of Projections in the Lozenge Tessellation|Natures of Projections in the Lozenge Tessellation]] for detail. One of the entities killed by The Projection in the latter chapters (mistakenly).

## Background characters
 
#### [[Dă Chỏ (deity)|Dă Chỏ (deity)]]

A giant mammoth of blue skin. Depictions of Dă Chỏ appear in the story as part of environmental worldbuilding. 




# Setup - Systems Framework

# Narrative Framework: The Arc of Escalating Insight

  
This document outlines the systems-based structural framework for the narrative set within the [[The Lozenge Tessellation|Lozenge T.]]. It utilizes environmental volatility to mask pacing and relies on internal psychological shifts to drive the plot.

  

## 1. The Core System (The Engine)

The narrative is driven by a nonlinear feedback loop rather than traditional linear plot points.

* **The Stock (The Metric):** The Projection's level of localized insight and compounded self-resentment.
* **The Balancing Loop (Avoidance):** The Projection's rigid adherence to their routine. When severe incongruities occur, they rationalize them using the perceived "internal logic" of the dreamscape to avoid confronting change.
* **The Reinforcing Loop (Volatility):** The rigid avoidance generates subconscious emotional friction. This friction amplifies the Tessellation's instability, causing more severe incongruities, which forces harder rationalization, creating more friction.
* **The Pacing Mechanism (Delays):** The tension is maintained through delayed feedback. The Projection executes an avoidant behavior and the environment remains in a deep NREM-like calm. The feedback hits abruptly later, plunging the environment into a highly volatile REM-like state.

## 2. The Micro-Structure: Chapter Loop (The Arrangement)

Each chapter represents a single Arrangement (a full sleep cycle) and must resolve its immediate physical tension before the awakening, while leaving the internal tension (insight/resentment) unresolved.

1. **The NREM Baseline:** The chapter begins in a stable zone. The Projection engages in their rigid, avoidant routine.
2. **The Incongruity:** A flaw appears in the zone's internal logic. The Projection attempts to ignore or rationalize it to maintain their balancing loop.
3. **The Delayed Escalation:** The suppressed emotional friction triggers a sudden shift. The environment fractures into a REM-like state where physical laws bend.
4. **The Localized Resolution:** The Projection survives the immediate physical threat of the REM phase, but only by recognizing a localized truth about their environment, resulting in a flash of insight.
5. **The Awakening Reset:** The excitation peaks and the dream resets. The external environment returns to a baseline, but the Projection carries the new self-resentment and insight into the next Arrangement via contextual bleed.

## 3. The Macro-Structure: The Zone Collision Arc

The overarching plot tracks the accumulation of the Projection's isolated flashes of insight and the breakdown of their avoidant routine.

* **Act I: The Bubble (Early Chapters)**
    * Restricted entirely to Zone 1.
    * The Projection is completely unaware of their Nature and blind to the conceptual gaps.
    * Focus is on establishing the rigid routine and the first minor failures of the balancing loop.

* **Act II: The Bleed (Middle Chapters)**
    * The Projection is forced into overlapping zones.
    * Insight gained from surviving REM phases allows them to witness discontinuity and conceptual gaps.
    * Avoidance mechanisms fail completely. Self-resentment reaches critical mass as the "Mug" (forced identity/routine) shatters.

* **Act III: The Convergence (The Climax)**

    * Self-resentment converts into courage.
    * Instead of rationalizing the severe incongruities, the Projection intentionally triggers a catastrophic excitation.
    * This deliberate act of lucidity collapses the boundaries, pulling all previously visited zones into a simultaneous, fractal collision in Zone 1.



# Setup - The Zone 1 Blueprint

# Act I Setup: The Zone 1 Low-Stakes Blueprint

## 1. The Psychological Paradox

* **The Desire:** A latent need for connection.
* **The Defense (The Curse):** A perceived cosmetic flaw that makes the Projection feel unworthy.

## 2. Chapter 1: The Success of Avoidance

This chapter establishes the deep NREM baseline. The physical stakes are deliberately low. The tension is purely psychological and atmospheric.

1. **The Prospect of Horror:** The Projection is preparing for or engaging in the early stages of the activity. The activity requires mutual trust and is terrifying to the Projection strictly because of the vulnerability involved.
2. **The Minor Incongruity:** An opportunity for genuine connection, communication, or vulnerability presents itself during the routine.
3. **The Evasion (Focus):** Paralyzed by their internal conflict, the Projection uses "Focus." They intentionally deaden the experience, look away, or perform a rote action to bypass the moment of vulnerability.
4. **The Hollow Victory:** The avoidance works flawlessly. The environment remains perfectly calm. There is no immediate physical danger, no REM shift, and no catastrophic failure. The activity concludes safely.
5. **The Lingering Void:** The chapter ends not with a cliff-hanger of physical danger, but with a quiet, unsettling emptiness. The Projection is physically safe, but emotionally isolated.

## 3. Chapter 2: The Delayed Cost

The physical and environmental consequences of Chapter 1's emotional suppression begin to manifest here, ramping up the tension.

1. **The Time Skip/Roadblock:** The environment presents a physical anomaly (e.g., sudden moss overgrowth, a structural shift) indicating that time behaved abnormally while the Projection was Focused in Chapter 1.
2. **The Curse Advances:** The Projection notices their cosmetic "curse" has visibly worsened. This is the delayed physical manifestation of the self-resentment generated by their evasion in the previous chapter.


# Setup - The Zone 1 Return Blueprint


# Act III Resolution: The Zone 1 Return Blueprint

This final section serves as the ultimate proof of Kjè's character arc. The environment and the physical stimuli are identical to Chapter 1, but Kjè’s internal response mechanism has been fundamentally rewired. 

## 1. The Waking World Resolution
* **The Second Bleed:** As the fractal collision of Zone 7 settles into the Zone 1 baseline, a final, brief flash of the waking world occurs.
* **The Act of Courage:** The Dreamer is shown executing an act of genuine self-acceptance or courage, directly contrasting the pathetic choice to throw away the plushie. This waking-world resolution stabilizes the dreamscape's foundation.

## 2. The Recontextualized Catalyst
* **The Identical Setup:** The narrative resumes precisely where Chapter 1 ended: *"The recorder still had the record function engaged."* Lem, identifiable by his specific color scheme and accessories, sits beside Kjè.
* **The Glitch:** The battery drains, and the audio mangles into the exact same stuttering loop: *"...gthing’s fine y felt off day off now-no-o-o-o-o..."*
* **The Insight Applied:** In Chapter 1, Kjè's internal logic interpreted this as an apocalyptic loss of control. Now, armed with the insight from Zone 7, he correctly identifies it as a simple mechanical failure. The fear of external punishment is gone.

## 3. The Consolidation (Shared Silence)
* **The Death of the Proxy:** The device clicks off. The loop ends. 
* **The Absence of Panic:** Kjè does not sob. He does not view the death of the device as the severing of his identity. 
* **True Connection:** Kjè recognizes that the music and the worth reside within him. He simply looks up at the sky and smiles. He shares this quiet, unforced moment with Lem. The environment remains perfectly calm, not because of avoidant "Focus," but because his internal psychological friction has ceased.


# Setup - The Zone 2 Blueprint

# Act II Initiation: The Zone 2 Blueprint

## 1. The Waking World Catalyst
* The environment introduces an element that clearly parallels the malfunctioning possession from Chapter 1, hinting at the Dreamer's waking-world instability.

## 2. The Deployment of Meekness
* Faced with a localized threat, Kjè actively deploys his new mechanism. He exaggerates his short stature or feigns helplessness to manipulate a partner entity into handling the danger.

## 3. The Severing
* The connection with the entity is abruptly broken. 
* **The Objective Reality:** The break is caused by the external volatility of the Tessellation (the Dreamer's interference).
* **The Flawed Perception:** Kjè completely misses the external cause. He is certain his manipulation was discovered and caused the abandonment.

## 4. The Cementing of the Loop
* This specific, devastating loss convinces Kjè that he is inherently broken. Instead of abandoning the manipulation, the guilt drives him to double down, believing he must become even more insidious so he is never "caught" again.


# Setup - The Zone 3 Blueprint

# The Zone 3 Blueprint

## 1. The Environmental Reflection

* **The Bleed:** The narrative begins with Kjè experiencing the physical manifestation of his Chapter 2 deception. The environment scales up drastically. 
* **The Initial Delusion (Victimhood):** Kjè perceives this not as shrinkage, but as the world becoming impossibly vast and out of reach. This reinforces his victim complex. He uses this physical barrier to justify his continued isolation.

## 2. The Incongruity

* Kjè attempts to navigate a previously manageable obstacle, but his altered scale makes it impossible. 
* He tries to use his weaponized meekness (from Chapter 2) on an Extra or the environment, but because he is *actually* physically insignificant now, the manipulation fails completely. He is ignored or brushed aside.

## 3. The Psychological Shift (The Friction)

* Stripped of his ability to manipulate, his internal logic begins to fracture.
* He transitions from viewing the vastness as a *barrier* to viewing it as a *cage*.
* **The Dangerous Thought:** The idea surfaces, unbidden: *Is this happening because of what I did? Is this punishment?*

## 4. The REM Trigger

* The moment Kjè brushes against accountability, the internal friction spikes drastically. 
* His avoidant nature violently rejects the thought, causing a massive surge in systemic volatility. 
* The Tessellation fractures under the pressure of this cognitive dissonance. The environment violently resets (REM shift), abruptly ending the chapter.


# Setup - The Zone 7 Blueprint

# Act III Climax: The Zone 7 Blueprint (The Shattered Mirror)

## 1. The Environmental Paranoia
* The Tessellation actively mirrors Kjè’s insidious nature. Every interaction feels like a trap. Stripped of his ability to softly manipulate his way out of danger, he is backed into a corner of absolute paranoia.

## 2. The Climax (The Fractal Collision)
* **The Paranoid Strike:** In a desperate bid for control, Kjè commits his final, unredeemable act of violence against Lem. 
* **The Loss of Control:** The Tessellation violently rejects Kjè. The ground falls away, catching him in a terrifying free-fall.
* **The Inverted Focus (The Insight):** Suspended in this uncontrollable descent, Kjè uses his "Focus" mechanism, turning it inward. The focus is so deep it pierces the dreamscape. He sees the truth of his form: he is a projection of a treasured childhood giraffe plushie, representing fragility and the Dreamer's identity.
* **The Reality Bleed (The Severing):** Through the focus, Kjè (and the reader) witnesses the waking world. 
    * The Dreamer is in another room. 
    * His mother asks if she should throw the toy away. 
    * The Dreamer responds: "Yes, please, I can't do this myself." 
    * A visual cue explicitly shows the narrator/reader that this is an act of ultimate avoidance—an attempt to sever and kill a part of his own identity.
* **The Rescue & Convergence:** Despite being broken by Kjè's attack, Lem intervenes, catching the falling Projection. The realization that he is dependent on the thing he tried to destroy acts as the catastrophic excitation. The boundaries of Zone 7 shatter, violently pulling all previous zones into a simultaneous collision back to Zone 1.

## 3. The Return (Zone 1 / Epilogue)
* **The Lesson Learned:** Kjè awakens back in the Zone 1 baseline. The environment is stable. 
* **The Second Reality Bleed:** A brief, final glimpse into the waking world occurs. It shows the Dreamer executing an act of true courage and self-acceptance (a direct contrast to the avoidant choice made earlier).
* **Consolidation:** Kjè remains alive and present in the dreamscape. Despite the Dreamer's past pathetic choice to throw the toy away, the memory and identity it represents live on in the psyche. Kjè transitions from a "curse" into a functional, consolidated symbol of self-worth.


# Setup - World Integration

# Synthesized Narrative Framework: The Arc of Escalating Insight

This document integrates the systems-based narrative framework with the specific world-building mechanics of the [[The Lozenge Tessellation|Lozenge T.]].

## 1. Core Systems & Environmental Volatility

The narrative engine relies on the friction between the Projection's avoidance and the environment's reactivity.

* **The Threshold of Volatility:** The NREM-like baseline is maintained solely by apathy and routine. The exact threshold that shifts the NREM zone into a highly volatile REM state is the Projection experiencing intense emotion, frustration, or a sudden realization.
* **The "Focus" Trade-off:** "Focus" is the ultimate expression of the Projection's avoidant balancing loop. By focusing intensely to overcome a specific obstacle, the Projection becomes completely unaware of the surrounding environment.
* **Delayed Feedback (Time Skips):** Time perception is broken. While the Projection uses Focus and ignores the environment, the environment ages or mutates rapidly in response to the suppressed emotional friction.
* **Physical Roadblocks:** The time skipped during Focus results in severe incongruities. When focus breaks, the Projection is blindsided by aggressive environmental mutations (e.g., sudden moss overgrowth, grotesque structural anomalies like "tumors") that physically impede their routine.

## 2. Contextual Bleed (False Memory/Deja Vu)

Events from previous Arrangements carry over, even if the Projection is unaware of the cycle. This "deja vu" is manifested purely through physical markers before the Projection gains conscious insight:
* **Muscle Memory:** The Projection reaches for objects, weapons, or handholds that existed in previous iterations but are missing in the current NREM zone.
* **Conditioned Flinching:** The Projection instinctively panics or flinches at specific sensory details (a smell, a sound) that consistently precede the violent shift into the REM state.
* **Environmental Echoes:** The Projection discovers physical marks (e.g., gouges in stone) that perfectly match the dimensions of an unremembered injury or lost tool.

## 3. Character Baseline: Form vs. Function

* **Feral Discrepancy:** Dominant life forms are animalistic. To avoid caricatures, the Projection's physical feral form must directly contrast with their underlying psychological Nature (e.g., a fragile prey animal form housing a highly aggressive, analytical function). This constant friction serves as a baseline stressor and a built-in incongruity.

## 4. The Micro-Structure (Chapter/Arrangement Loop)

Each chapter must resolve its immediate physical tension before awakening, leaving internal tension unresolved.

1.  **Baseline:** Projection engages in a localized, rigid routine within a stable NREM zone.
2.  **Incongruity:** A flaw appears. The Projection uses "Focus" to avoid or bypass it.
3.  **Delayed Escalation:** Focus breaks. The environment has mutated severely (time skip/physical roadblock). The suppressed emotion crosses the threshold.
4.  **REM Shift:** The environment violently fractures into the REM state.
5.  **Localized Resolution:** Not necessarily a triumphant resolution. Something must resolve, have local consequence, answer to the questions posed earlier, propose new ones, etc. The final resolution should unambiguously result in courageous (uncharacteristic) behaviour. One question should remain: is this change for the better, or is it erasure? 
6.  **Awakening Reset:** The excitation peaks. The environment resets, but the insight and compounding self-resentment carry into the next cycle.

## 5. The Macro-Arc (Zone Collision)

* **Act I (The Bubble):** Restricted to Zone 1. Total unawareness. Focus is on routine and the first failures of avoidance.
* **Act II (The Bleed):** Overlapping zones. Gained insight allows the Projection to witness gaps. Focus fails. Self-resentment compounds as the routine shatters.
* **Act III (The Convergence):** Self-resentment transforms into courage. The Projection intentionally triggers catastrophic excitation. This deliberate lucidity forces all zones into a simultaneous, fractal collision in Zone 1 to resolve the core psychological conflict.
