# Asaxi Vowel Interaction — A Phonological Critique

**Brief:** assess *real* vowel harmony in Asaxi — phonological feature-spreading among vowels within and across roots, suffixes and prefixes — using standard typological frameworks (Turkic backness/rounding, Uralic, Niger-Congo / African ATR), and disregarding the simplified "warmth vowel-harmony" gloss in the working prompts.

**Method.** Close reading of `00_Phonemes`, `22_Phonotactics & Euphony`, `00_Noun Classification (Gender)`, `47_Morphological Reduction (Class Suffixes)`, `09_Adjectives`, `16_Constitution vs Simile`, `31_Adverbial Nuance (Warm vs. Cold)`, `61_Prosody`, `65_Source Doctrine`, plus an automated scan of **432 class-bearing noun entries** in the `Lexicon/` for vowel content vs. assigned class.

**Headline finding.** Asaxi has **no vowel harmony** in the technical sense. What the documentation calls "vowel harmony" is a **two-term grammatical-gender (noun-class) concord system** — *warm* vs *cold* — whose exponents happen to be vowel-bearing suffixes (`-nă`/`-nýj`, `onă`/`onýj`). The conditioning factor is the noun's **lexical/semantic class**, not the phonological features of its vowels. The lexicon confirms this statistically: a root's vowel content does **not** predict its class. Several genuine *segmental* vowel-interaction processes do exist (coalescence, glide formation, devoicing, haplology, a phrase-final backing rule), but none is harmony.

## 1. Feature Geometry & Inventory

### 1.1 The vowel system (from `00_Phonemes`)

**Monophthongs (9 qualities):**

| Rom. | IPA | Height | Backness | Round | Tense/ATR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| i | /i/ | high | front | − | +tense |
| ý | /ɪ/ | near-high | front | − | −tense (lax) |
| e | /e̞/ | mid | front | − | (mid) |
| è | /ə/ | mid | central | − | reduced |
| ě | /ɚ/ | mid | central | − | rhotic |
| ù | /ɯ/ | high | back | **−** | +tense |
| o | /o̞/ | mid | back | **+** | (mid) |
| a | /a/ | low | front/central | − | — |
| á | /ɑ/ | low | back | − | — |

**Diphthongs (6):** ă /aɪ/, å /au̯/, ů /uu̯/, ë /eɪ/, ỏ /ou̯/, ő /oɪ/.
**Syllabic-nasal nuclei:** mm /m̩/, nn /n̩/, nŋ /ŋ̩/ (occupy the V-slot; `61_Prosody`).

### 1.2 What feature could drive harmony?

A harmony system needs **one privative/binary feature** with enough contrastive pairs to spread. Asaxi supplies none:

- **Rounding** is almost non-contrastive in the monophthongs. The only rounded monophthong is /o̞/; the high back vowel is **/ɯ/ (unrounded)** — there is **no /u/**. With no front-rounded series (/y ø/) and effectively one rounded vowel, a **Turkic-style rounding harmony is structurally impossible**.
- **Backness** has no aligned pairs. For backness harmony you need front~back partners agreeing in height/round (i~ɯ, e~o, …). Asaxi has /i/ and /ɯ/ (which differ only in backness — a lone candidate), but /e̞/~/o̞/ also differ in rounding, and the low vowels /a/~/ɑ/ are a backing allophony, not a harmonic pair (see §2.4). One pair cannot anchor a system.
- **ATR / tense-lax** has exactly **one** clean contrast: **/i/ ~ /ɪ/** (i ~ ý). There is no /u/~/ʊ/, no /e/~/ɛ/, no /o/~/ɔ/. A Niger-Congo-style ATR harmony cannot run on a single pair.

### 1.3 The "warm/cold" sets are not a natural class

The prompts assign: **warm** = {a, i, e, o, á, ă, å, ỏ}; **cold** = {ý, ù, è, ě, ë}. Tested against features, these groupings are **incoherent**:

- *warm* mixes front (i, e), back (o, á), low (a), and round (o) — no shared feature.
- *cold* mixes front lax /ɪ/, **back** /ɯ/, central /ə ɚ/, and a **tense front diphthong** /eɪ/ (ë) — no shared feature.

The nearest charitable generalization is **peripheral/tense (warm) vs centralized/lax (cold)** — but /ɯ/ (peripheral back) sits in "cold", and /eɪ/ (peripheral front) sits in "cold", breaking even that. **Conclusion:** the warm/cold split is **not phonologically definable**; it is a semantic/sound-symbolic grouping. No phonological feature is available to harmonize, so §§2–4 are, strictly, tests the system was always going to fail.

## 2. Root-Internal Phonotactics

### 2.1 Is there static (root-internal) harmony? — No.

A vowel-harmony language shows **static co-occurrence restrictions** inside underived roots (Finnish *pöytä* not \**poytä*). Asaxi has no such restriction, stated or observed. Scanning 432 class-bearing noun roots and tagging each vowel warm/cold:

| | warm-vowels only | cold-vowels only | **mixed (disharmonic)** | no V |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **warm class** (n=203) | 139 (68%) | 10 (5%) | **54 (27%)** | 0 |
| **cold class** (n=229) | 151 (66%) | 14 (6%) | **63 (28%)** | 1 |

**27% of roots overtly mix "warm" and "cold" vowels** — e.g. `hùso` (ù+o), `vèno` (è+o), `cùgo`, `cőcő`, `gajýnnshá`, `dăjý`, `fùko`, `gùŕo`, `dåmëno`. A genuine harmony system would flag these as ill-formed or repair them; Asaxi does neither. Disharmonic roots are simply normal.

### 2.2 The decisive test: class is statistically independent of vowel content

If "warmth" were vowel-driven, class would be **predictable** from the root's vowels. It is not. The rate of *warm-vowels-only* is **68% inside the warm class** and **66% inside the cold class** — essentially identical. Vowel profile carries **no information** about class. (The high baseline simply reflects that the "warm" vowels /a i e o/ are the highest-frequency phonemes; "cold-only" roots are a mere 5–6% in *both* classes because /ý ù è ě ë/ are rare segments — a frequency fact, not a harmonic one.)

### 2.3 Counterexamples that break the claim outright

- **Cold class with all-bright vowels:** **`ihjo`** /iço/ "bone" (i, o), **`gosi`** /ɡosi/ "salt" (o, i), **`gogo`** /ɡoɡo/ "pebble/gravel" (o, o), **`bao`**, **`daono`**, **`daogo`** — 151 such nouns. Their vowels are 100% "warm", yet they take the **cold** suffix (`ihjonýj`). Phonological harmony cannot produce this; **semantic class can** (bone/salt/stone are inert → cold).
- **Warm class with all-dark vowels:** **`swë`** "scales", **`të`**, **`těněn`**, **`bù`**, **`fù`**, **`jëvè`** — warm class despite containing only "cold" vowels.

These minimal counterexamples show the suffix is selected by **gender**, not by the stem vowels — the defining property of concord, the negation of harmony.

### 2.4 What *is* there at root level

Real, observed vowel phonotactics are about **syllable structure and hiatus**, not harmony:

- **Doubled-vowel length** (`toonă`, two morae) — `22`, `61`.
- **Phrase-final /a/ → [ɑ]** under the terminal fall (`natăka` → [natɑkɑ]) — `61`. This is the **only** genuine vowel-quality *assimilation* in the language, and it is **prosodically** triggered (boundary tone), not harmonic.
- **Vowel devoicing** between voiceless C (`shěso` → [ʃ̩so]) — reduction, not harmony.

## 3. Morphophonological Alternations (Affixation)

### 3.1 The one alternation, and what conditions it

Exactly one alternation pattern in Asaxi looks harmony-like — the **warm/cold concord paradigm**:

| Slot | Warm | Cold | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simile/quality adjective | **-nă** | **-nýj** | `09`, `16` |
| Definite determiner | **onă** | **onýj** | `00 Noun Class`, `02` |
| Demonstrative determiner | **ponă** | **ponýj** | determiner pages |
| Degree prefix | **nă-** | **nýj-** | `nă- / nýj- (Degree Prefix)` |
| Adverbial nuance | warm | cold | `31_Adverbial Nuance` |

Its conditioner is the **head noun's lexical class**, i.e. morphosyntactic agreement, proven by §2.3: `ihjo` (warm vowels) → **`ihjonýj`** because *bone is cold*. No phonological feature of the stem participates. This is **concord**, realized by a vowel-bearing exponent — not vowel harmony.

### 3.2 Directionality

In harmony terms one could *describe* the pattern as **progressive / stem-controlled** (the root controls a following affix) — but this is an artifact of the affixes being suffixal. There is **no spreading**: the feature does not propagate vowel-to-vowel, it is a single agreement value copied onto a designated slot. The prefixal members (`nă-`/`nýj-`) show the same value appearing **before** the root, which would be *regressive* if it were harmony — incoherent for a spreading account, perfectly normal for **agreement** (agreement is direction-agnostic). This internal "both directions" behavior is itself evidence against a harmony analysis.

### 3.3 Affix typology: alternating vs invariant

A harmony language makes **most** vowel-bearing affixes alternate (Turkish *-lar/-ler*, *-dı/-di/-du/-dü*). Asaxi does the opposite — the overwhelming majority of affixes are **invariant**, with a single fixed vowel regardless of the root's vowels or class:

- **Invariant (non-alternating):** verbalizer **-ů**, thing **-no**, place **-ŕo**, creature **-shá**, structure **-kam**, zone **-gă**, muscle **-båbå**, fusing **ga-**, privative **fů-**, plural **-a**, every tense prefix (**o-, no-, ko-, na-, mi-, hù-, zè-, pa-, sỏ-**), every locative prefix (**na-, va-, xa-, pù-, ba-, hù-, pa-, ỏ-**), the bridges **-n-/-w-/-x-**.
- **Alternating (concord only):** the warm/cold set in §3.1.
- **Dominant affixes (harmony sense):** none. (The "dominant" morphemes of `61_Prosody` — `ná`, `xă`, `ă`, k-interrogatives — are **High-tone** dominants on the *pitch* tier, an unrelated axis.)

That a creature suffix is always `-shá` (never \**-shè* after a dark root), a plural always `-a`, a verbalizer always `-ů`, is dispositive: **affixes do not harmonize to the root.** The system fails the most basic diagnostic of vowel harmony.

### 3.4 Genuine affix-level vowel processes (not harmony)

The real vowel interactions at morpheme boundaries are **hiatus resolution**, feature-blind:

- **Coalescence** `a + i → ă` (**ga + i → gă**), and prefix-V + `i`-root → diphthong (**zè + ijù → zëjù**, **no + ijo → nőjo**) — `22 §C`.
- **Glide formation** `ů → w` before a vowel (**fů + ai → fwă**, *bafwai*) — `22`.
- **Haplology** of identical morae (**vivi + gavi → vigavi**; **ŕoŕo + -no → ŕono**) — `22`.

These target **syllable well-formedness** (avoid hiatus / identical-mora sequences); they are indifferent to warm/cold and do not propagate any feature. They are exactly what a language *without* harmony uses instead.

## 4. Neutrality, Transparency, & Opacity

These notions are **defined only within a harmony domain** (transparent = skipped by spreading; opaque = blocks and re-initiates a domain). Asaxi has **no harmony domain and no spreading**, so the concepts are, strictly, **inapplicable** — there is nothing for a vowel to be transparent *to*.

Mapping the diagnostics onto what exists:

- **No domain is defined.** The natural candidate domain would be the **phonological word** (one pitch peak; compounds unify to a single peak — `61 §7`). Even granting that domain, no feature spreads across it (§2–§3).
- **De-facto "opacity" everywhere.** Because affix vowels never alternate (§3.3), every affix vowel is, trivially, "opaque" in the weak sense of *never participating* — but this is invariance, not phonological blocking.
- **Diphthong incoherence undercuts any neutral-vowel account.** A real system designates neutral vowels (Finnish *i, e*). Asaxi's diphthongs are assigned to warmth in ways that contradict their components: **ă /aɪ/ is "warm"** though it ends in the "cold" nucleus /ɪ/; **ë /eɪ/ is "cold"** though it is a tense front diphthong built from "warm" /e/; **ő /oɪ/ and ů /uu̯/ are unassigned**. If warmth were phonological, a diphthong's class would follow its vowels; it does not.
- **Syllabic-nasal nuclei** (mm, nn) bear **no warmth value** at all (what suffix does a nasal-nucleus root such as `mmrå-` select?). An undefined nucleus class is impossible in a real harmony system, where every nucleus must carry the harmonic feature or be explicitly neutral.

**Conclusion:** transparency/opacity cannot be assessed because the prerequisite — feature-spreading within a domain — does not exist.

## 5. System Cohesion & Typological Plausibility

### 5.1 Against the harmony prototypes

| Diagnostic | Turkic / Uralic / ATR | **Asaxi** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A phonological feature spreads | yes (back, round, ATR) | **no feature available (§1)** |
| Roots are internally harmonic | yes (strong tendency) | **no; 27% disharmonic (§2)** |
| Affixes alternate to agree with root | yes (most/all) | **no; affixes invariant (§3.3)** |
| Conditioner is phonological | yes | **no; lexical gender (§2.3, §3.1)** |
| Neutral/transparent vowels defined | yes | **n/a (§4)** |
| Domain defined | yes (word) | **undefined (§4)** |

Asaxi matches the prototype on **zero** diagnostics.

### 5.2 What the system actually is

The warm/cold mechanism is a textbook **two-class grammatical gender / noun-class concord** system. Semantically it is **animacy/heat-based** ("animated or radiating heat = warm; otherwise cold", `00 Noun Class`) — typologically ordinary (cf. animate/inanimate genders; Algonquian; the common/neuter split). Its morphology — a head noun controlling the shape of dependent adjectives and determiners (`-nă/-nýj`, `onă/onýj`) — is **agreement**, exactly like Bantu (Niger-Congo) **noun-class concord** or Indo-European gender agreement. That the agreement exponents contain different vowels (ă vs ý) is incidental: those vowels are **morphemic exponents of a class feature**, not targets of a phonological rule.

So the system is **internally coherent — as gender concord.** The concord itself is applied regularly across the grammar (adjective, determiner, degree, adverb). The incoherence is purely **terminological/architectural**: the documentation also asserts that *vowels themselves carry warmth* (a phonological claim) and lets that "lean" guide coining. Those two ideas are independent, and the lexicon shows the phonological one is **false in practice** (§2.2). The conflict is not in the mechanics of agreement; it is in **calling agreement "vowel harmony."**

### 5.3 Note on overall phonological maturity

This is not a naïve phonology. `61_Prosody` specifies a mora-timed **pitch-accent** system (lexical accent, dominant-H morphemes, downstep, boundary tones, compound peak-unification) that is coherent and typologically credible. `22` has a real hiatus-resolution toolkit. The *only* weak joint is the mislabeled "harmony." The fix is therefore cheap and local (see §6).

### 5.4 Two repair paths

1. **Re-label (recommended, low-cost):** call the warm/cold system **gender/noun-class concord**; demote "vowel warmth" to an explicit, optional **phonaesthetic tendency** (sound symbolism), not a phonological rule, and state that affix vowels do **not** harmonize. This matches the data with near-zero rewriting.
2. **Actually build harmony (high-cost):** pick one real feature with paired vowels (most plausibly **ATR**, which would require *adding* /ʊ ɛ ɔ/ to partner /ɯ̞.../e o/, and a true /u/), enforce root harmony, and make the invariant affixes alternate (`-shá`→`-shá/-shè`, `-no`→`-no/-ně`, `-a`→`-a/-è`, etc.). This is a major retrofit and would collide with existing fixed lexemes.

## 6. Error Report (contradictions, missing rules, gaps)

**A. Category / architecture**

1. **Mislabel:** "vowel harmony" denotes a **noun-class concord** system. This is a category error propagated from `00 Noun Class` into the working prompts ("Warmth vowel-harmony (lean, not law)").
2. **Conflated systems:** the docs simultaneously claim vowels *have* warmth (phonological) and that nouns *have* a warmth class controlling agreement (morphosyntactic). The first does not predict the second (§2.2) — they should be separated.

**B. Inventory cannot support harmony**

3. **No harmonic feature.** Warm/cold cross-cuts backness, height, rounding and tenseness (§1.3).
4. **Rounding harmony impossible:** one rounded monophthong (/o̞/); high back is **/ɯ/**; no /u/, no front-round series.
5. **ATR/tense harmony unsupportable:** only one tense/lax pair exists (/i/~/ɪ/).
6. **Diphthong class incoherence:** ă /aɪ/ tagged warm though it contains /ɪ/; ë /eɪ/ tagged cold though tense-front; **ő, ů unassigned** to warmth.
7. **Syllabic-nasal nuclei** (mm, nn, nŋ) have **no warmth assignment** — undefined behavior for nasal-nucleus roots (`mmrå-`).

**C. Empirical mismatches**

8. **Class ⊥ vowels:** warm-vowel-only rate ≈ 68% (warm class) vs 66% (cold class); 151 cold-class nouns are all-bright (`ihjo`, `gosi`, …).
9. **Disharmonic roots ≈ 27%** and are unremarked/unrepaired; root-internal harmony is neither stated nor observed.

**D. Missing rules (gaps the docs never specify)**

10. **Do affix vowels harmonize?** Never stated. (Answer from data: **no** — they are invariant. This should be made explicit.)
11. **Harmony domain** undefined (root? phonological word? clitic group?).
12. **Directionality** undefined; the prefixal `nă-/nýj-` vs suffixal `-nă/-nýj` would be contradictory under a spreading account (§3.2).
13. **Neutral / transparent / opaque vowels** undefined.
14. **Compound class assignment** unspecified: when a warm root + cold root combine (e.g. `ŕago` = ŕa[warm] + go[stone]), which class results, and on what principle (head? rightmost? semantics?)?
15. **Loanword integration** unspecified: how is class (and any "warmth lean") assigned to Polish/Russian/Japanese loans (`xăto`, `fnè`, `stè`)?

**E. Orthography/IPA hygiene (minor, but relevant to a vowel audit)**

16. **`ů` IPA inconsistent:** chart lists Arpabet-style "uw" in the IPA column; elsewhere /uu̯/. Pick one (likely /uu̯/ or /ʉ/).
17. **`sh` overloaded:** romanization "sh" maps to both /ɕ/ (also written *si/ś*) and /ʃ ~ ɹ̠̊/; "r" maps to /ɹ/ *and* /ɹ̠˔/. Overloaded graphemes complicate any statement about which segments pattern together.
18. **Allophony vs harmony:** the phrase-final **/a/→[ɑ]** backing (`61`) is the lone true vowel-quality assimilation and is **prosodic**; it should be explicitly distinguished from the (non-existent) harmony so future readers don't mistake it for one.

---

### One-line verdict

Asaxi's "vowel harmony" is a **well-behaved binary gender/noun-class concord** wearing a phonologist's label it has not earned: there is **no harmonic feature, no harmonic root constraint, no affix alternation to root vowels, and no harmony domain** — only agreement, plus a set of ordinary hiatus-repair processes. Re-label it as concord (and optionally keep "vowel warmth" as declared sound-symbolism), or commit to a real feature and make the whole affix system alternate.
