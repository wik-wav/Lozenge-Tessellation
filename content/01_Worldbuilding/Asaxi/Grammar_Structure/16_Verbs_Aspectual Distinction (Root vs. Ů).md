## Grammatical Concept: Aspect, Roots, and the `-ů` Durative

Asaxi verbs fall into two **derivational** classes — **root** (primitive) and **`-ů`** (derived) — and that morphology also carries **lexical aspect**. One mapping is load-bearing:

> **A verb ending in `-ů` is a durative *Activity*. A bare root is *punctual* by default.**

Everything below follows from that.

### 1. Root Verbs (punctual by default)

A closed class of ancient words for fundamental interactions. **By default they are Achievements — instantaneous events**, a change of state in a single moment.

- **xoxo** (depart) — the moment of leaving.
- **topù** (drop) — the moment of release.
- **ijo** (spot) — the moment of perception.

**Lexically durative roots.** A subset — chiefly **locomotion** and a few sustained physical acts — are inherently **durative Activities**. This is a lexical property recorded on the entry, *not* read off the shape: **haśù** (run), **aśù** (walk), **vůwù** (swim), **kŕama** (march). They behave as Activities with no suffix.

### 2. The `-ů` Durative (two sources, one meaning)

The Universal Verbalizer **`-ů`** always yields a **durative Activity**. It takes two kinds of input:

- **Denominal** — `[noun] (+ mode infix) + ů`: **shěso** → **shěsonů** (read), **jýn** → **jýnů** (chat). (Mode infixes: [[06_Verbs in Asaxi]].)
- **Deverbal — the durativizer** — `[punctual root] + ů` turns a one-moment event into its ongoing process: **ijo** (spot) → **ijonů** (to watch, to keep in view); **xoxo** (depart) → **xoxonů** (to be leaving, be on one's way out). (For *drop*, the established mass-process is the denominal **toponů** "to rain/fall.")

> **Revision from older canon.** The deverbal `-ů` was formerly **forbidden** — you had to supply an unrelated verb for the durative. It is now **licensed and productive**: `ijonů` is well-formed. Pre-existing suppletive verbs (e.g. **mëjonů** "gaze") stay valid as independent, more specific lexemes; the regular `[root]+ů` is simply the transparent durative.

The invariant holds either way: **anything ending in `-ů` is durative**, whether built from a noun or a root.

### 3. The Aspect Operators — two axes

Aspect beyond the lexical default is marked by **additive prefixes**, on two independent axes.

**Axis I — Punctual ↔ Durative** (how many of the event)

| Operator | Shift | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **`-ů`** (durativizer) | punctual → durative | one event → its ongoing process | `ijo` spot → **ijonů** watch |
| **[[na- (relational locative) (particle)\|na-]]** (iterative) | punctual → repeated | a rapid series of the event | `ijo` → **năjo** keep spotting / monitor |
| **[[tå- (Semelfactive Prefix)\|tå-]]** (semelfactive) | durative → single instance | one bounded occurrence | `jýnů` chat → **tåjýnů** utter once |

`na-` and `tå-` are **inverses**: `na-` multiplies a punctual into many; `tå-` collapses a durative into one. Each does its real work on the *opposite* base — a punctual root is already single, an Activity already extended — so the prefix is where the marked meaning lives.

**Axis II — Phase** (the boundary of the event)

| Operator | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[[ni- (particle)\|ni-]]** (inceptive) | onset — "begin to" | **nixoxo** I start to leave |
| **[[chå (particle)\|chå-]]** (completive) | result / finish — "fully, to completion" | **chåshěsonů** read fully; **chåxoxo** gone for good |

The axes combine freely: `năjo` (iterative), `tåjýnů` (semelfactive), `chåshěsonů` (completive), `nixoxo` (inceptive).

### 4. Coercion (aspect mismatch with the argument)

Pairing a verb of one aspect with an argument of the other gives a marked, meaningful reading — exploited, not avoided.

- **`topo topù.`** rain + drop(punctual) → "the rain dropped **once** / hit like a single object" (semelfactive coercion by the punctual root).
- **`shěso toponů.`** book + rain(durative) → "books are **continuously raining**", or in contex
